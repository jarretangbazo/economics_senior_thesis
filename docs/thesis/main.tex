        \begin{abstract}
            \singlespacing
            % Replace with your final abstract — 150-250 words
            This paper examines the effect of violent conflict on the
            educational attainment and school enrollment of children in
            Nigeria. Using data from the 2010 Nigeria Education Data Survey
            (NEDS) and geocoded violence data from the Uppsala Conflict Data
            Program (UCDP) spanning 1999--2010, I estimate OLS models with
            and without LGA fixed effects to identify whether exposure to
            violence at different stages of childhood affects the probability
            of school enrollment and the number of years of schooling
            completed. I find that exposure to violence during early childhood
            (ages 0--3) and preschool age (3--6) is associated with a
            reduced likelihood of current school enrollment, while the effects
            on total years of schooling are mixed and sensitive to model
            specification. Results confirm well-documented gender and
            urban-rural disparities in educational attainment. These findings
            motivate continued investment in school access and protection of
            education during conflict.

            \vspace{0.5em}
            \noindent\textbf{Keywords:} conflict, education, Nigeria, Boko
            Haram, school enrollment, UCDP, OLS, fixed effects
        \end{abstract}
